Installation stories

What actually happens between order and opening

The parts of a door replacement that decide whether the finished job holds up: the measurement, the delivery inspection window, and the first fitting.

Chapter one · The measurement

The rough opening decides everything after it

A door is ordered to the rough opening, not to the old slab. Width and height are taken at three points each and the smallest of the three governs; the diagonals are taken to confirm the opening is square before anything is bought.

The measuring guide

Chapter two · The delivery

The five-day window

Transit damage has to be raised inside the retailer's inspection window, which is why the unit is unwrapped and checked on the day it lands rather than on the day it goes in. Glass, frame faces and the bottom rail are the three to look at first.

The delivery checklist

Chapter three · The fitting

Shimmed square, or nothing else matters

A composite frame will hold the geometry it is set in. That is the advantage, and it is also the warning: an opening shimmed out of square stays out of square, and the weatherstrip will never close the gap it was not asked to close.

The installation checklist

Chapter four · The years after

What maintenance actually consists of

Wash the faces, keep the sill track clear, and check the weatherstrip once a season. There is no refinishing cycle, because there is no substrate underneath that needs the finish to survive.
